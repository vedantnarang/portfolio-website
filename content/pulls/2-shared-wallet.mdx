## Problem

Group expense splitting ends in the same mess every trip: pairwise IOUs multiply, and
"who owes whom" becomes unreconstructable. Naive Splitwise-style tracking stores debts
per pair; settling up fairly across a group of six means either trusting an app's black
box or doing graph math by hand.

## Approach

A **2D ledger model**: every expense writes ledger rows against canonical group members,
and settlement is computed as net positions rather than stored pairwise debt â€” the data
model resolves debts deterministically instead of accumulating them. Real-time state
syncs through Convex (conflict handling and multi-user updates handled by the platform's
transactional model rather than hand-rolled locking), with Clerk for identity.

The part worth reading in the codebase is the settlement derivation: how netting
interacts with partial payments, and why the ledger stays append-only so any balance
can be recomputed from history.

## Tradeoffs

- **Convex vs Postgres + WebSockets** â€” Convex collapsed sync/concurrency work to
  days at the cost of platform coupling. Right call for a weekend-scale product;
  wrong call for anything where query flexibility matters more than time-to-ship.
- **Compute-on-read balances vs materialized balances** â€” recomputing from the ledger
  is slower per view but impossible to drift from truth; correctness beat caching here.

## What I'd do differently

Settlement suggestions should batch on a window ("settle weekly") instead of firing on
every expense â€” churny micro-suggestions made the UX noisier than the problem deserved.
