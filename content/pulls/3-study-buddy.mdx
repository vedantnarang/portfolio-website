## Problem

Students study from messy inputs â€” photographed handwritten notes, PDFs, diagrams â€” and
want flashcards, quizzes, and summaries out the other side. Vision-capable models on
free tiers are exactly that: free, capable, and unreliable under rate limits.

## Approach

A multimodal pipeline with degradation as a first-class behavior. Uploads go to
Cloudinary; public URLs feed a **three-model fallback chain** over OpenRouter's
vision-capable models. If the primary model rate-limits or times out, the next takes
the request â€” extraction quality degrades gracefully instead of the feature failing.
Extracted text becomes structured study material (flashcards/quizzes/summaries)
persisted in MongoDB.

The interesting engineering is in the fallback semantics: what counts as "retry-worthy"
versus "give up", how partial extractions surface to users honestly, and keeping the
chain config-driven so models swap without touching pipeline code.

## Tradeoffs

- **Free model chain vs one paid model** â€” zero cost and real redundancy at the price
  of inconsistent output formats across providers. Normalization code pays that bill
  forever; see [#11](/issues/11), where I argue for collapsing it into a single paid
  model plus an explicit retry budget.
- **Server-side extraction vs client-side** â€” extraction needs the image bytes anyway,
  so doing it server-side keeps provider traffic (and keys) off the client entirely.
