### Problem

Team pages mixed data from different seasons without saying so. Three functions were involved:

- `get_team_position_history` defaulted to `p_season = '2025'` and hardcoded `competition = 'Premier League'` against a free-text season column.
- `get_team_analytics` read the WhoScored events table seven times with no season or competition filter.
- `get_team_formation` had the same unfiltered read.

### Why now

This one reached production. The position-history default was mine, from the team-trajectory graph I'd shipped in May; the analytics and formation functions were teammates'. It surfaced while scoping team pages to seasons. The function *looked* season-aware because it already had a season parameter — which is exactly why nobody noticed the frontend never passed one.

### Approach

A shared rule first: `null` means "latest season with data", never "all seasons". A forgetful caller then gets the right shape of answer for a possibly-wrong season — visible — instead of a silent multi-season blend that looks like working code.

- `0092` adds `team_default_scope()` and `get_team_seasons`.
- `0093` scopes position history and matches through the structured competition/season keys instead of the free-text hardcode. The old signature is dropped first: adding even fully-defaulted parameters creates a second overload, and the frontend's existing call fails with `function is not unique`.
- `0095` adds the season filter to all seven analytics reads and the formation read.

### Explicitly not doing

- The two league-wide materialized views (press height, fouls) are cross-team leaderboards, not team reads — left out of scope.
- `get_team_formation` was fixed but not wired up: nothing in the frontend calls it.

### Resolution

Shipped in 8a87e93 (`0092`–`0094`) and 37ca597 (`0095` plus the frontend season switcher).
