### Problem

Flickstat's expected-goals numbers come from two different providers' models. League xG is Understat's; Champions League and World Cup xG is BSD's. Each provider trains its own model on its own features, so a player's 0.4 xG in the league and 0.4 xG in the Champions League aren't measuring the same thing — and nothing on the site says so.

### Why now

Today the two only ever appear on separate pages, so the mismatch is latent rather than visible. That's the cheap moment to fix it: the next natural feature — comparing a player's league form with his European form, or opening the compare view to Champions League players — would put the two models side by side and present the difference as football.

### Approach

Both providers already give us what a baseline model needs, per shot: pitch coordinates, body part and situation (open play, set piece, penalty). Derive distance and angle from the coordinates, then fit one logistic regression over both providers' stored shots. Show it next to the provider figure rather than replacing it, labelled as ours, until it has been checked against a full season.

A logistic regression on shot geometry is a well-understood baseline, and it's honest about being one.

### Explicitly not doing

- Anything involving defensive pressure or possession sequences. The data isn't there, and pretending otherwise would produce a confident-looking number that's wrong.
- Removing the provider xG. It stays the default until the in-house figure has earned trust.

### Open question

The two providers use different coordinate systems — Understat's 0–1 pitch versus BSD's own — and the mapping between them is the first thing to get right. A wrong xG figure is worse than no xG figure.
