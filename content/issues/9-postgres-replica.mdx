### Problem

I've used Postgres through Supabase, which means I've used it without ever operating it. I can write the queries, design the schema and ship the migrations, but I've never configured streaming replication, never watched replica lag climb under load, never handled a failover. In an interview I could describe replication accurately from reading; I couldn't tell you what it feels like when it goes wrong.

### Why now

The gap between "knows about" and "has operated" is the main thing separating me from the engineers I want to be working with. Reading about WAL shipping is not the same as having a replica fall behind and having to work out why.

### Approach

Two Postgres instances on cheap VPS boxes, streaming replication between them, a load generator pointed at the primary, and a read-only copy of Flickstat-shaped traffic pointed at the replica. Then deliberately break things: kill the primary, saturate the network, fill the disk. Write up what happened.

### Explicitly not doing

Putting it in front of real users. Flickstat's traffic doesn't remotely need a read replica, and a learning setup shouldn't be load-bearing.

### Priority is honestly low

This is learning, not user value. It's here because I'd rather be explicit that it's a P3 than quietly pretend it's urgent work.
